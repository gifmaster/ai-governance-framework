# AI Council charter: [Organisation name]

## Purpose

The AI Council makes sure [Organisation] uses AI in line with its AI policy, the law and its risk appetite, without slowing down low-risk use.

## Members

| Role | Name | Vote |
|---|---|---|
| Chair (AI Governance Lead) | | ✓ |
| Business representative | | ✓ |
| DPO or privacy lead | | ✓ |
| IT or security | | ✓ |
| Legal (standing or on call) | | Advisory |
| HR (for use cases affecting employees) | | Advisory |

> Small organisations: three members is enough. An external adviser can fill the legal or privacy seat.

## Decisions the council makes

- Approves use cases with **enhanced** or **strict** [governance intensity](../framework/03-oversight-scale.md#governance-intensity) at gates G2 and G3
- Decides disputed classifications
- Approves exceptions to the AI policy, with a time limit
- Reviews the AI register every quarter
- Proposes changes to the AI policy to management

Decisions on **light** and **standard** use cases are delegated to the AI Governance Lead and reported to the council.

## How it works

| | |
|---|---|
| Regular meetings | Quarterly, 60 minutes |
| Ad hoc | Within [10] working days when a strict use case needs a decision |
| Quorum | Chair plus two voting members |
| Decisions | By consensus. If not possible, the chair decides and the dissent is recorded. |
| Conflict of interest | A member who owns the use case being decided does not vote on it |
| Records | Decisions and reasons are recorded in the AI register |

## Standing agenda

1. New use cases for decision
2. Changes to existing use cases (purpose, data, vendor, oversight level)
3. Incidents since last meeting
4. Register review: overdue reviews, tools found outside the register
5. Regulatory updates
6. Training status

## Reporting

The chair reports to [management] [twice a year] on: number of use cases by intensity, incidents, overdue reviews and upcoming regulatory changes.
