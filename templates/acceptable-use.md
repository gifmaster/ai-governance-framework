## The short version

1. Use the AI tools on our approved list ([link]). Ask before you use anything else.
2. Never put [confidential], customer or personal data into a tool that is not approved for it.
3. You own what you send, publish or decide. AI can draft. You check.
4. Tell people when they are dealing with AI, where we have said you must.
5. If something goes wrong, tell [AI Governance Lead] the same day. Nobody gets in trouble for reporting in good faith.

## Green, amber, red

| | What it covers | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **Green: go ahead** | Approved tools used for what they are approved for. Drafting, summarising, translating, brainstorming and coding help on material that is not confidential. | Use it. Read and check the output before you use it. |
| **Amber: follow the conditions** | Personal or customer data in a tool approved for that data. Output that goes to customers or is published. AI-generated images, audio or video. [Confidential] data, special categories of personal data (health, ethnicity, religion and similar) or passwords in tools not approved for them. Decisions about hiring, pay, performance, credit or access to services based on AI output alone. | Do not do it. If you think there is a good reason, raise it with [AI Governance Lead]. |

## Before you rely on an output

- **Is it true?** AI tools state wrong facts with full confidence. Check numbers, names, quotes, sources and legal points.
- **Is it fair?** Look for output that treats groups of people differently without reason.
- **Is it ours to use?** Do not pass off someone else's text, images or code as ours.
- **Would I sign it?** If your name is on it, you are responsible for it.
